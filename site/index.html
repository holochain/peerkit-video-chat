Private peer-to-peer video calls

A small, focused showcase for PeerKit, a TypeScript peer-to-peer framework. Live audio, video, and text chat that flow directly between the people on the call whenever the network allows.

Free & open source · Linux · macOS · Windows

What it does

You meet in a room: a named space you create or join just by typing its name. Everyone in the same room is on the same call and shares its text chat. Rooms are temporary — when the last person leaves, the room and everything said in it are gone.

Live audio & video

Real-time group calls. Your audio and video go straight to the other people in the room whenever the network allows.

In-the-moment text chat

Chat alongside the call. Messages reach only the people in the room at the time — nothing is saved, replayed, or shown to anyone who joins later.

Private to your group

An organization runs its own instance, and its members join the rooms inside it. Each instance is a closed network — outsiders can't take part.

Direct, with a fallback

Calls connect directly when they can, and automatically take a relayed path when a direct one isn't possible — so they keep working on restrictive networks.

Run it yourself

The app finds peers through a lightweight server you can host on your own infrastructure. It helps people connect; your audio and video don't pass through it.

Resilient connections

If a call drops, the app automatically tries to restore it — several times, in increasingly thorough ways — before giving up.

How it works

A few technical terms below; the short version is that calls go peer-to-peer, and the server is only there to help peers find and reach each other.

  1. 1

    Discover

    Each app connects to a PeerKit relay — a rendezvous point — and learns which other members are online and which rooms are active.

  2. 2

    Connect

    Peers link up over PeerKit's libp2p network — directly to each other where the network permits, falling back to the relay otherwise. The WebRTC setup messages (SDP and ICE) travel over that link, so they go direct whenever the peers do.

  3. 3

    Call

    Media flows over WebRTC. ICE uses STUN to find a direct path between peers; when none exists, an optional (but recommended) TURN relay forwards the encrypted media as a fallback.
